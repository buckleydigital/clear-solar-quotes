Payback

What actually decides how fast solar pays for itself

Payback gets quoted as a single number, but it moves a long way depending on how your household actually uses power.

Self-consumption beats export, by a lot

Power you use as it is generated saves you the full retail rate. Power you export earns a feed-in tariff, which is typically a fraction of that. Running the dishwasher, pool pump or washing machine during daylight is the single biggest lever most households have, and it costs nothing to pull.

Your tariff structure

Time-of-use and flat tariffs change the maths completely. Worth checking which one you are on before comparing quotes.

When you are home

An empty house all day exports most of what it makes. A home office or shift worker changes the numbers dramatically.

Whether you add a battery

A battery lifts self-consumption but adds cost. It usually lengthens simple payback while cutting your bill further.

System longevity

Panels degrade slowly and predictably. A well-installed system keeps producing well past the point it has paid for itself.

The incentive is taken off at the quote

The federal small-scale incentive is almost always applied by the installer as a discount on the price you are quoted, rather than something you claim back yourself later. Its value depends on where you live and the expected output of the system, and it steps down on a set schedule, so the same install is generally worth less the longer it is left.

Plain english

The jargon, decoded

Quotes are full of terms that sound interchangeable and are not. Here is what they actually mean.

kW vs kWh
kW is the size of your system, its maximum rate of generation. kWh is the amount of energy produced or used over time. Your system is sold in kW, your bill is charged in kWh.
Inverter
Converts the DC power your panels produce into the AC power your house uses. It is the component that most often needs attention first, so its warranty matters.
String vs microinverter
A string inverter runs the whole array through one unit. Microinverters sit under each panel, which helps when part of the roof gets shaded but costs more.
Feed-in tariff
What your retailer pays for power you export to the grid. It varies by retailer and has generally fallen over time, which is why using your own power matters more than it used to.
Degradation rate
How much output a panel loses each year. A lower rate over 25 years is a large part of what you pay for with premium panels.
Export limit
Some networks cap how much you can send back to the grid. It can affect the system size you are allowed to install, so it is worth asking about early.
